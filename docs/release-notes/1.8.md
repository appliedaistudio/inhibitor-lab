# Inhibitor Service 1.8 Release Notes
## Overview
Version 1.8 expands inhibitor-lab’s governance baseline and makes the compliance surface materially more useful for real-world policy translation. The release is centered on the update to `docs/supported-regulations.md`, which now becomes a broader “Supported Regulations and Standards” reference and clarifies how the Inhibitor handles binding regulations, recognized assurance standards, and public guidance frameworks.
The result is a clearer source-of-truth document for teams turning legal, safety, and institutional requirements into operational Inhibitor rules, escalation paths, and audit expectations.

## Highlights
  * Renamed and reframed the core governance document from a regulations-only list into a broader **Supported Regulations and Standards** reference.
  * Clarified that Inhibitor support covers not only detection and preventative constraints, but also audit-event recording and escalation of ambiguous, high-risk, or guidance-only findings for human review.
  * Distinguished binding regulations from standards and guidance frameworks, making it clearer that non-binding sources are supported through conformance monitoring, risk detection, and oversight support rather than legal determinations.
  * Added a new **Education & Research AI Governance** section based on UNESCO guidance for generative AI in education and research.
  * Introduced education- and research-specific governance coverage across data privacy, age-appropriate access, institutional auditing, ethical and pedagogical validation, terms-of-use awareness, harmful or unlawful use monitoring, and long-term learning impact review.
  * Added explicit source references and tightened adjacent-document links so governance readers can move directly from policy scope into API behavior, policy-to-rule examples, privacy guidance, and deployment guidance.

## Adoption Guidance
  * Use `../supported-regulations.md` as the primary governance scope document when mapping policies into Inhibitor rules, escalation workflows, and audit review criteria.
  * Treat the new language around standards and guidance frameworks as especially important for internal governance programs: it helps teams distinguish between legally binding obligations and operational oversight expectations.
  * For education, research, and institutional AI deployments, use the UNESCO-backed additions as a policy-authoring starting point for privacy, learner protections, institutional review, and harmful-use monitoring.
  * Pair the updated governance scope with `../inhibitor-api.md` and `../policy-rule-examples/README.md` so prose requirements connect cleanly to runtime enforcement and rule design.
  * Review `../gdpr-compliance.md` and `../global-edge-deployment.md` alongside this release when locality, privacy, and cross-border handling are part of the deployment design.

## Looking Ahead
  * Continue expanding the governance reference with source-backed standards and public guidance that can be operationalized into deterministic review and enforcement patterns.
  * Add clearer mappings from major governance domains into example rule shapes so teams can move faster from policy interpretation to implementation.
  * Extend sector-specific governance coverage where institutional review, human escalation, and long-horizon risk assessment matter more than simple pass/fail compliance checks.
